\section{Methodology}

The 60-feature observation $o_t^{(60)} \in \mathbb{R}^{60}$ used by the
team's PPO baseline is a point-statistic snapshot: per-tick means, last- and
last-three-tick views, and episode-historical means of bid, cost, and
win-rate signals. It does not encode three classes of information that are
recoverable in closed form from the per-tick history cache the simulator
already maintains: (i) \emph{time derivatives} of those statistics, (ii)
\emph{higher-order moments} of the within-tick competitor-bid distribution,
and (iii) \emph{opponent-aggression proxies} that ratio-encode auction
outcomes. Since the simulator's $47$ competitors are unobserved, every
non-Markov signal the policy might use must be surfaced into $o_t$
explicitly; this extension adds such features at the observation layer
while leaving the action space, transition model, reward, and PPO
objective untouched.

We define the market-aware observation
\begin{equation}
  o_t \;=\; \big[\,o_t^{(60)};\;\phi^{(1)}_t;\;\phi^{(2)}_t\,\big]
  \;\in\;\mathbb{R}^{98},
\end{equation}
where $\phi^{(1)}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{20}$ (\emph{Tier~1}) selects $20$
named statistics that the simulator's history cache already maintains per
tick but that $o_t^{(60)}$ does not surface (per-slot win counts and
exposure rates, raw conversion counts, CPA-exceedence rates, and episode
cumulatives), and $\phi^{(2)}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{18}$ (\emph{Tier~2}) is a
set of features derived in closed form from the same cache via three
primitives: a length-$W$ least-squares slope $\mathrm{slope}_W$, a length-$W$
sample standard deviation $\mathrm{std}_W$, and an episode-history $z$-score
$z_t(x)\!=\!(x_t-\bar x_{1:t})/\mathrm{std}_{1:t}(x)$, all with $W{=}5$. The
$18$ Tier~2 features are organised into six categories that target the
three blind spots above: competitor-bid distribution shape,
opponent-aggression proxies, ad-slot dynamics, regime $z$-scores, signed
momentum of win rate / ROI / conversion rate, and a budget-pacing
residual; their definitions are listed in the Appendix.

The augmented observation is paired with the same clipped-PPO surrogate,
the same actor and critic MLP architectures (apart from the input layer
width, which grows from $60$ to $98$), the same $20$-period rollout
schedule on training periods $7\!-\!26$, and the same $10\mathrm{M}$-step
budget as the $o_t^{(60)}$ baseline. Because the modification is confined
to the observation layer, this extension is by construction orthogonal to
those that act on the reward (Lagrangian shaping), on rollout sampling
(score-weighted reweighting), or on the policy class (GRU temporal
encoder, LLM policy), and could in principle be composed with any of them.

\section{Results}

We evaluate the trained policy on the held-out period $27$ using the
$48$-advertiser sweep protocol (one deterministic rollout per advertiser at
that advertiser's true \texttt{(budget, CPAConstraint)}). The market-aware
policy attains a mean evaluation score of
$\mathbf{24.85 \pm 2.09}$ (s.e.m., $n{=}48$), reported in
Table~\ref{tab:market-aware-eval} alongside the $16$- and $60$-feature PPO
baselines from the team's summary table.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \small
  \caption{Held-out period-27 advertiser-sweep evaluation. Baseline numbers
  are from the team-wide extension comparison; the market-aware run uses
  the same PPO hyperparameters and $10\mathrm{M}$-step budget.}
  \label{tab:market-aware-eval}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
    \toprule
    Observation & Dim. & Sweep score (mean $\pm$ s.e.m.) \\
    \midrule
    PPO baseline (obs\_16)         & $16$ & $24.8$ \\
    + $60$-feature state           & $60$ & $27.0$ \\
    + Market-aware ($o_t^{(98)}$)  & $98$ & $24.85 \pm 2.09$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The market-aware observation is statistically indistinguishable from the
$16$-feature PPO baseline on this evaluation and underperforms the
$60$-feature configuration by roughly one s.e.m. The training-time signal
on the $20$ training periods, however, is healthy: the per-rollout score
logged during training rises from $\approx\!18$ at $1\mathrm{M}$ steps to
$\approx\!25.4$ averaged over the final $1\mathrm{M}$ steps, with
$\text{cost\_over\_budget}\!\approx\!0.93$ (slight under-pacing) and
$\text{target\_cpa\_over\_cpa}\!\approx\!1.05$ (slightly inside the CPA
target). The training trajectory plateaus around $3\mathrm{M}$ steps and
remains flat through $10\mathrm{M}$, suggesting that the additional
$38$ features did not introduce instability but also did not unlock
further gains beyond what the $60$-feature observation already captures on
the training distribution.

The most parsimonious explanation for the train/test gap is a
\emph{generalisation} failure: a wider observation enlarges the input
layer of the actor and critic MLPs and provides more degrees of freedom
along which the policy can specialise to the training periods $7\!-\!26$.
Several Tier~2 features (windowed slopes, $z$-scores, ratios) are also
high-variance scalar transforms of the same underlying signals already
present in $o_t^{(60)}$, which can amplify estimation noise without adding
information that survives the train/test distribution shift to period
$27$. Two natural follow-ups are (a) a permutation-importance pass on the
trained model to identify which Tier~2 features actually carry signal in
the training distribution, followed by a leaner $o_t$ that retains only
those keys, and (b) regularisation aimed at the wider input layer (e.g.\
input dropout or an L$2$ penalty on the first-layer weights of the actor
and critic). Both are deferred to future work; the present extension
should be read as evidence that simply expanding the observation vector
along plausible market-regime axes is not sufficient on this benchmark.

\section{Appendix}

\subsection*{A.1\ \ Windowed primitives}

For any tracked scalar signal $x_{1:t}$ and window length $W$, with
$\bar k = (W-1)/2$ and $\bar x = \tfrac{1}{W}\sum_{k=0}^{W-1} x_{t-W+1+k}$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{slope}_W(x_t) \;=\;
  \frac{\sum_{k=0}^{W-1}(k-\bar k)(x_{t-W+1+k}-\bar x)}
       {\sum_{k=0}^{W-1}(k-\bar k)^{2}},
  \qquad
  \mathrm{std}_W(x_t) \;=\;
  \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{W}\!\sum_{k=0}^{W-1}(x_{t-W+1+k}-\bar x)^{2}}.
\end{equation}
The slope is the closed-form OLS estimate against an integer time index;
the episode $z$-score $z_t(x)=(x_t-\bar x_{1:t})/\mathrm{std}_{1:t}(x)$
uses cumulative mean and std over the episode prefix, with
$\mathrm{std}_{1:t}$ floored at a small $\varepsilon$ to guard against
constant prefixes.

\subsection*{A.2\ \ Tier~1 surfaced statistics (20 keys)}

Per-slot win counts $\{\text{bid\_success\_count\_slot\_}j\}_{j=1,2,3}$ in
last/last-three/historical views; per-slot exposure rates
$\{\text{exposure\_mean\_slot\_}j\}_{j=1,2,3}$ in the same three views;
last-three and historical raw conversion counts; last/last-three/historical
CPA-exceedence rates; and the episode cumulatives
$Y_t,\,C_t,\,\mathrm{CPA}_t$. These quantities are already maintained by
the simulator's per-tick history update, so Tier~1 is implemented as a
selection-only change to the JSON observation key list with no code
change to the environment.

\subsection*{A.3\ \ Tier~2 derived features (18 keys)}

Below $\mathrm{lwc}_t$ denotes the within-tick mean of the least winning
competitor cost (the auction floor faced by the agent), $b_t$ the agent's
mean submitted bid at tick $t$, $p_t$ the mean predicted conversion
probability over the tick's impression batch, $\mathrm{wr}_t$ the win rate,
and $C_t,B,T_{\!\text{left}}$ the cumulative spend, total budget, and
remaining time fraction.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep 2pt
  \item \textbf{Competitor distribution shape (7).}
        $\mathrm{slope}_5(\mathrm{lwc})$ and $\mathrm{std}_5(\mathrm{lwc})$
        capture trend and volatility of the bid floor; the coefficient of
        variation $\mathrm{std}_5/\mathrm{mean}_5$ separates absolute from
        relative volatility; $z_t(\mathrm{lwc})$ locates the current tick
        within the episode's own scale; the right-tail width
        $\mathrm{lwc}^{(01\%)}_t-\mathrm{lwc}^{(10\%)}_t$ and ratio
        $\mathrm{lwc}^{(\max)}_t/\mathrm{lwc}^{(\mathrm{mean})}_t$ expose
        ``whale'' bidders whose presence the baseline mean smooths out;
        $\mathrm{slope}_5$ of the percentile gap tracks whether the tail
        is widening.
  \item \textbf{Opponent-aggression proxies (2).} Because competitor bids
        are unobservable, we infer opponent stance from auction outcomes:
        $\alpha^{\mathrm{opp}}_t = \mathrm{lwc}_t / p_t$ (price the market
        clears per unit of predicted conversion) and the agent's own
        competitiveness $\beta_t = b_t / \mathrm{lwc}_t$.
  \item \textbf{Slot dynamics (3).} The cost ratio
        $c^{(1)}_t/c^{(3)}_t$ between the top and floor ad slot, the
        analogous exposure ratio, and the $\mathrm{slope}_5$ of slot
        concentration. Together they signal whether premium ad real estate
        is being contested or vacated.
  \item \textbf{Regime $z$-scores (2).} $z_t$ of pv-volume intensity and of
        win rate, locating the current tick within the episode's empirical
        distribution rather than against an absolute scale.
  \item \textbf{Momentum / acceleration (3).} $\mathrm{slope}_5$ of
        $\mathrm{wr}_t$, of the ROI proxy $p_t/\mathrm{lwc}_t$, and of the
        per-tick conversion rate.
  \item \textbf{Budget-pacing residual (1).}
        $\rho_t = C_t/B - (1-T_{\!\text{left}})$, with $\rho_t>0$ indicating
        cumulative spend ahead of elapsed time and $\rho_t<0$ indicating
        underpacing.
\end{itemize}
